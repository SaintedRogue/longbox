---
title: Offline reading
---

Longbox installs as an app, and an installed app that needs a server to show you a book
you already own is not much of an app. Download issues to the device and read them with
no connection at all.

## Installing first

Offline reading depends on the app being installed, because that is what gives it a
service worker and durable storage. Install from your browser's address bar — "Install
app" in Chrome and Edge, **Share → Add to Home Screen** on iOS.

<Callout type="info">
	**HOUSE RULES** — After installing, **reload once**. The service worker only takes control of a
	page it did not itself load, so the first visit after installing is still served by the network.
	Skip the reload and your first offline attempt will come up empty.
</Callout>

## Downloading a book

Open a book and use **Download for offline** in its action menu. Progress is shown as it
copies, and **Downloads** in the sidebar lists everything you hold locally, with the
option to remove any of it.

Downloaded books are readable from the Downloads list whether you are online or not.

## Reading progress survives the trip

This is the part worth understanding, because it is where offline reading usually goes
wrong elsewhere.

Progress made while offline is **queued on the device**, not discarded and not silently
lost. When the connection returns, the queue is sent and your position on the server
catches up. Read three issues on a train with no signal and your shelf is correct by the
time you are back in range.

The queue survives the app being closed — it is stored, not held in memory.

## Storage

Downloads and the progress queue live in your browser's own storage for the site, which
means:

- **Clearing site data removes them.** Downloads are a local copy, not a backup.
- The operating system may reclaim storage under pressure. Rare, but possible on a nearly
  full phone.
- Each browser and each installed copy is separate — a book downloaded in Chrome is not
  present in Safari.

## What is not offline

Browsing, search, metadata and covers for books you have **not** downloaded all need the
server. Offline mode is about reading what you have deliberately taken with you, not a
full mirror of the library.
