title: Collections
---

A [series](/docs/guides/fundamentals/series) is a folder. A **collection** is a shelf of
books that belong together without sharing one — the same run, scattered across a flat
directory or split between folders.

## Where they live

Open a library, go to **Series**, and switch the toggle from **Series** to
**Collections**. They use the same card as a series deliberately: a collection is a
shelf of books exactly like a series is, it just isn't backed by a folder.

## Building them

Collections are detected rather than created. From the Collections view, run
**Detect collections**: Longbox groups books that share a series according to their
metadata, and leaves genuinely standalone books alone.

Nothing moves on disk. Detection reads metadata and writes groupings — your files stay
exactly where they are.

<Callout type="info">
	**HOUSE RULES** — Detection needs metadata to group on. A library of files named `v01.cbz` inside
	well-named folders already has series and will produce few collections; a flat pile of loose files
	with good embedded metadata will produce many.
</Callout>

## Where a grouping came from

Each collection records how it was formed:

| Source       | Meaning                                                    |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Metadata** | Grouped on the series recorded in the book's own metadata. |
| **Filename** | Grouped on a series name parsed out of the filename.       |
| **Manual**   | You created or corrected it.                               |

A book you have placed by hand is not moved again by a later detection run.

## Collections, series, and the organizer

Three related tools, easy to confuse:

- **Series** — the folders on disk. Longbox does not invent these.
- **Collections** — groupings on top of your files, changing nothing on disk.
- The [**organizer**](/docs/guides/features/organizer) — actually _moves_ loose files
  into folders, changing the library on disk.

Reach for a collection when you want the shelf to read correctly and the files left
alone. Reach for the organizer when you want the files themselves tidied.
