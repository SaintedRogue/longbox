title: Search
---

Search finds books by anything written about them — not just the filename.

## What a term matches

One term is checked against all of these at once:

- The book's **name** and its metadata **title**
- **Summary**, **publisher** and **series**
- **Writers**, **characters** and **story arc**
- Its **tags**

A term that parses as a number _also_ matches the issue number, but exactly: searching
`1` finds issue 1, not #10, #11 and #21. That is deliberate — substring-matching a
number column makes issue search useless.

## Several terms narrow, they do not widen

Terms are **AND**-ed. `claremont phoenix` returns books that match `claremont`
somewhere **and** `phoenix` somewhere — not necessarily in the same field. So a
Claremont-written issue with Phoenix in the summary matches, which is usually what you
meant.

Adding a term always narrows the result. If you get nothing back, take a term away.

## What it deliberately ignores

**The file path.** It looks tempting and it is a trap: an absolute path carries its
library's mount prefix, so a library at `/mnt/comics` would return every book in it for
the term `comics`.

The noisier credit fields — genres, colourists, letterers, teams — are also left out of
bare search, so a common surname does not drown the result. They are all still
reachable through the filter picker, where you have asked for that field by name.

## Search and filters together

Search is combined with whatever filters are active rather than replacing them. A
filtered shelf stays filtered while you search inside it — which is how you find one
book among your omnibuses without leaving the
[Omnibuses tab](/docs/guides/features/omnibus-shelf).

<Callout type="info">
	**HOUSE RULES** — This is substring matching, not a ranked full-text index. Results come back in
	your chosen sort order, not by relevance, and there is no stemming: `writing` will not find
	`writer`.
</Callout>
