The calendar is your pull list: one Sunday-aligned week of issues that are expected,
day by day, for the series you follow.

## Finding it

**Calendar** in the sidebar. Two views sit at the top right:

| View             | Shows                                                     |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **My pull list** | Only series you follow. The default.                      |
| **All series**   | Everything Longbox has release data for, followed or not. |

Arrows step a week at a time; **Today** comes back.

## Filling it

The calendar does not read your files — nothing in a `.cbz` knows what ships next
month. It needs two things, and it will look empty until both are true.

<Steps>

<Step>
### Match your series to a provider

Release dates arrive from a metadata provider, which means a series has to be matched
to that provider's record of it first. See
[metadata fetching](/docs/guides/integrations/metadata-fetching). A library with no
matches has no calendar.

</Step>

<Step>
### Let the calendar sync run

A background job pulls upcoming releases for your matched series. It is scheduled — see
[background jobs](/docs/guides/fundamentals/background-jobs) — and you can run it on
demand from the server's job settings.

</Step>

<Step>
### Follow the series you care about

Follow a series from its page menu. Anything you follow shows up under **My pull
list**; **All series** ignores following entirely.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout type="info">
	**HOUSE RULES** — An empty week is usually sparse matching rather than a quiet month. Switch to
	**All series** first: if that is empty too, the gap is upstream of the calendar — few series
	matched, or the sync has not run.
</Callout>

## Which providers supply dates

[League of Comic Geeks](/docs/guides/integrations/metadata-fetching/providers) is the
strongest source here, because a weekly release list is the thing it is built around.
[Metron](https://metron.cloud) also carries dates. A provider that knows your series
but publishes no schedule contributes nothing to this page.

## What a day holds

Each of the seven days is present whether or not anything ships, so the week reads as a
week. An entry shows the series, the issue number and its cover where one is known —
clicking through goes to the series.
